## Quick verdict

Before anything else, the honest baseline both companies' ads gloss over: **identity-protection services monitor and help you remediate. Neither Identity Guard nor LifeLock can prevent identity theft.** No service can. They tell you faster and help clean up — they don't stop the crime. The single most protective thing you can do is **freeze your credit**, free at all three bureaus, and you do that yourself regardless of which service you pick.

With that established:

**Pick Identity Guard if** you want competent monitoring at a lower entry price, you don't want the surprise of a big year-two renewal, and you don't need antivirus or a VPN bundled in. It's the value pick.

**Pick LifeLock if** you'd buy **Norton 360** anyway (its security suite is bundled into most plans) or you place real value on LifeLock's long-established restoration brand and US-based specialists. It's the ecosystem pick.

Both are reviewed in depth on this site — see our [Identity Guard review](/identity-guard-review/) and our [LifeLock review](/lifelock-review/) for the tier-by-tier detail. For most budget-minded buyers, Identity Guard is the easier recommendation; LifeLock earns its place inside the Norton world.

## Pricing — the clearest difference

| Plan | Identity Guard | LifeLock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry tier | Value, ~$7-9/month | Standard, ~$9-12/month intro |
| Mid tier | Total, ~$15-20/month | Advantage, ~$19-23/month intro |
| Top tier | Ultra, ~$20-25/month | Ultimate Plus, ~$30-35/month intro |
| Renewal | More consistent year over year | Commonly jumps 50-100% after year one |
| Bundled security | None (monitoring-focused) | Norton 360 (antivirus, VPN, password manager) |

**The honest read.** Identity Guard's entry price is its calling card — often the cheaper way to get *something* watching your data — and it doesn't carry LifeLock's renewal-shock reputation. LifeLock's introductory pricing is competitive too, sometimes lower at the very bottom, but the **year-two jump** is the gotcha: a plan that hooked you at $9.99 can renew near $17-20, with the product unchanged and the promo simply expired. Over two years, Identity Guard is frequently the better pure-price value. LifeLock's price buys you the Norton bundle, which Identity Guard doesn't offer at all.

## Monitoring and tiers — what you actually get

Both cover the core checklist: SSN and credit monitoring, dark-web surveillance, financial-account alerts, and identity-theft insurance. The important shared catch is **bureau coverage**.

**Identity Guard** reserves **three-bureau** credit monitoring for its top **Ultra** tier; Value and Total lean on a single bureau. **LifeLock** reserves three-bureau monitoring for **Advantage and Ultimate Plus**; Standard typically monitors one bureau. Either way, the cheap tier on both services leaves credit gaps, because a thief can open accounts using whichever bureau a given lender pulls. **If you want genuine credit coverage, you're buying the top tier on both** — and at that level, LifeLock's Ultimate Plus is pricier than Identity Guard's Ultra, while adding the Norton suite.

So: at the top tier, the comparison is "Identity Guard Ultra (cheaper, monitoring-only)" vs. "LifeLock Ultimate Plus (pricier, includes Norton 360)."

## The AI claims — Identity Guard's "Watson" vs. LifeLock's pitch

Identity Guard's headline differentiator is being "**powered by IBM Watson**" AI. Here's the straight answer: it uses AI/machine-learning to scan data and surface alerts, which is real — but functionally similar to what LifeLock and virtually every competitor do with their own ML and rules engines. The "Watson" branding makes it sound like a unique moat; in practice, what you experience is alerts in an app, same as anywhere.

LifeLock doesn't lean on a single AI brand name; it leans on **brand recall and Norton's security heritage**. Neither company's "smarts" should be the deciding factor — both do competent automated monitoring. Decide on price, bureau coverage, and whether you want the Norton bundle, not on whose AI sounds more impressive.

## Identity theft insurance — same fine print on both

Both advertise "**up to $1 million**" (more on top tiers). On **both** services this is a **third-party-underwritten insurance policy** with per-incident caps, category sublimits (stolen funds, personal expenses, and legal fees are separate buckets), exclusions, and a claims process. It's not a guaranteed refund of any loss, and it sits behind your bank's own fraud protections (Regulation E, card zero-liability), which pay first. This is a wash — treat the headline number as marketing on both sides and read the actual benefits summary.

## The Norton bundle — LifeLock's real differentiator

This is the clearest reason to choose one over the other. **LifeLock bundles Norton 360** — antivirus, VPN, and a password manager — into most plans. If you'd buy security software anyway, that bundle is genuine added value and the strongest argument for LifeLock over Identity Guard.

**Identity Guard doesn't bundle antivirus or a VPN.** It's monitoring-focused. If you don't want a security suite — or you already have one — you're not paying for software you won't use, which is part of why its pricing is leaner. If you *do* want all-in-one identity-plus-security, note that **Aura** (Identity Guard's corporate cousin) is built for exactly that; see our [Aura vs LifeLock comparison](/aura-vs-lifelock/).

## Restoration and support

**LifeLock** has the longer, more famous restoration track record — US-based specialists who've handled identity crises for years, and the brand recall that comes with it. **Identity Guard** also provides victim-assistance and restoration support from an established operator (the Intersections / Aura lineage), competent if less heavily marketed.

If a battle-tested restoration name gives you peace of mind, that tilts toward LifeLock. If you mainly want solid monitoring and reasonable support at a lower price, Identity Guard does the job.

## Who Identity Guard is right for

- You want **competent monitoring at a lower price** and the Value/Ultra tiers appeal to you.
- You **don't want antivirus or a VPN** bundled — just your identity watched.
- You'd rather avoid LifeLock's **year-two renewal jump**. (Detail in our [Identity Guard review](/identity-guard-review/).)

## Who LifeLock is right for

- You'd buy **Norton 360** anyway and want identity monitoring on the same bill.
- You value LifeLock's **established restoration brand** and US-based specialists.
- You'll track the renewal date and not over-buy the discounted top tier. (Detail in our [LifeLock review](/lifelock-review/).)

## The honest call

For a budget-minded buyer who wants solid identity monitoring without a renewal surprise and doesn't need a security suite, **Identity Guard is the easier recommendation** — lower entry price, no renewal-shock reputation, established operator.

**LifeLock wins for the Norton crowd** — if you'd buy antivirus anyway, the Norton 360 bundle and the famous restoration brand justify the higher (and rising) price. Just diarize the renewal and don't buy Ultimate Plus at the discount only to wince at year two.

And on both: do the free thing first — **freeze your credit at all three bureaus.** It costs nothing and does the single most protective job either service charges for. Then choose the monitoring layer that fits your price tolerance and your interest in the Norton bundle.

## Final recommendation

For most people: **Identity Guard** is the value pick — cheaper, no renewal-shock reputation, solid monitoring from an established operator. Read the [full Identity Guard review](/identity-guard-review/) for the tier detail.

**LifeLock** is the call if you live in the Norton world or specifically want its restoration brand — just track the renewal date and don't over-buy the discounted top tier. Our [full LifeLock review](/lifelock-review/) covers the tiers and the renewal trap.

Whichever you choose, freeze your credit first. It's free, and it does more to prevent new-account fraud than anything either service sells.
